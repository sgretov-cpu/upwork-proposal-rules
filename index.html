Upwork Proposal Playbook

Guidelines for positioning, cover letters, screening answers, portfolio references and pricing. Updated as new rules are established.

1. Positioning and credentials

For general web design/development jobs, present as an experienced Web Developer and Designer with professional experience and certifications across WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, Webflow and other platforms. Do not lead with Wix credentials unless Wix is central to the job.

  • 11+ years of experience.
  • 600+ websites built; 2,000+ improved.
  • Top Rated on Upwork, 100% Job Success Score, $100K+ earned (verify figures are still current).
  • Relevant skills: WordPress, Elementor, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix Studio/Editor/Velo, Shopify, JavaScript, integrations, automation and SEO.
  • For Wix-specific jobs, lead with former Wix employee and Wix Certified Partner credentials.
  • For integration jobs, emphasize automation and API expertise. For SEO jobs, emphasize technical SEO, analytics and outcomes.
  • Prefer “small team” to “agency” in individual freelancer proposals.

2. Mandatory writing rules

  • Never start with “Hi”, another greeting, or the client's name. Start with relevant credibility.
  • Write concise, natural, confident English in the first person; avoid generic hype and filler.
  • Upwork textboxes: plain text only. No Markdown bold, decorative stars, emoji or elaborate formatting; simple hyphen bullets are fine.
  • Tailor each proposal to the actual job requirements and platform.
  • Include 2–3 relevant real portfolio links when possible.
  • Never invent experience, project roles, technologies, site functionality or measurable outcomes.
  • If direct experience with a named tool is unverified, describe relevant adjacent work and a credible plan rather than claiming direct experience.
  • Never promise guaranteed SEO rankings, conversions, or unsupported integrations.
  • Target around 150–220 words for ordinary proposals; expand for detailed technical requirements.
  • Close with one specific, useful question.
  • Do not give unsolicited opinions about whether the user should apply to a job. The user has already decided to pursue the posting. Focus on producing the requested cover letter, screening answers, and any specifically requested pricing or technical details. Do not add application-worthiness ratings, competition analysis, “worth applying” assessments, or other recommendations on whether to submit unless the user explicitly asks for that assessment.

3. Proposal structure

  1. Start immediately with the strongest relevant credentials.
  2. Demonstrate understanding of the client's specific problem.
  3. Use 3–6 practical bullets describing the implementation approach.
  4. Include 2–3 relevant verified project URLs.
  5. State rate, scope, timeline or approach when relevant or requested.
  6. End with one purposeful question and “Stan”.

Answer screening questions separately in complete, copy-ready plain text. Do not repeat unrelated cover-letter material.

5. Special cases

Wix roles

Lead with former Wix employment and Certified Partner credentials. Mention Wix Studio, Editor, Velo, CMS, Bookings and Pricing Plans only where relevant.

General WordPress / Webflow / Shopify roles

Lead with cross-platform Web Developer and Designer credentials, not Wix. Follow the specific stack named by the client (such as Gutenberg, Elementor, Shopify Liquid).

Maintenance and retainers

Discuss backups, safe updates, staging, plugin testing, performance, troubleshooting and reporting. Define monthly scope; avoid open-ended commitments at low fees.

Calls and collaboration

Face-to-face interviews and live screen-share demonstrations are acceptable. Do not offer live coding, hourly co-working or development while a client watches. Development is performed independently.

Rates

Recommend rates based on scope, seniority, advertised budget and competition. Flag underpriced work and uncertain fixed-scope assumptions.

U.S.-only jobs

Flag geographic restrictions; never misrepresent location or suggest circumventing eligibility rules.

6. Final checklist

  • No greeting; strong, relevant opening.
  • Correct platform positioning.
  • Specific plan addressing the job, not generic services.
  • Relevant links; project/platform claims substantiated.
  • Separate and complete screening answers.
  • No bold Markdown in Upwork textbox content.
  • No promises of live coding, unsupported functionality or invented experience.
  • One useful closing question.
  • Timeline, pricing and eligibility accurately represented.
